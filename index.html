1. IT Security Policy

  • Use individual accounts and long, unique passwords. Never share passwords.
  • Enable multi-factor authentication where available and lock your screen when away.
  • Do not open suspicious links or attachments. Report suspected phishing immediately.
  • Install only approved software and do not disable security tools or updates.
  • Keep business, payroll and customer information in approved storage and share it only with authorised people.
  • Use approved staff Wi-Fi. Guest Wi-Fi must remain separate from internal systems.
  • Report lost devices, malware, unauthorised access and accidental data disclosure immediately.

2. Acceptable Use Policy

  • Use AFC devices, email and network mainly for authorised business purposes.
  • Do not access illegal, offensive or harassing material using company systems.
  • Do not install unapproved applications, browser extensions or remote-access tools.
  • Do not bypass security controls, access another person’s files or connect unknown devices.
  • Do not send confidential company information to personal email or unauthorised cloud services.
  • Respect software licences and copyright. Report misuse or security concerns to IT.

3. Equipment Acquisition and Approvals

  1. Submit a request describing the business need, quantity, location and required date.
  2. IT checks compatibility, security, warranty, support and licensing.
  3. Finance confirms budget availability and total cost.
  4. The IT Manager approves technical suitability; the authorised budget holder approves spending.
  5. The authorised purchaser obtains quotations and issues the purchase order.
  6. IT inspects delivered items, configures them securely, applies an asset tag and updates the asset register.
  7. Finance matches the invoice to the approved order and receipt.

Do not order equipment before approval, except under a documented emergency process.

4. Service Desk / Help Desk Procedure

  1. Contact the approved service desk using the company’s confirmed email or telephone details.
  2. Provide your name, branch, device or service affected, symptoms, time noticed and business impact.
  3. Keep the ticket number and provide updates when requested. Never send your password.
  4. IT classifies and prioritises the issue, performs safe checks and escalates complex incidents.
  5. IT records the fix and confirms that the service works before closing the ticket where possible.

5. Disaster Recovery

If a major incident occurs

  1. Protect people first and report the incident to the IT Manager or service desk.
  2. If malware is suspected, disconnect the affected device from the network if safe to do so. Do not delete evidence or attempt unauthorised repairs.
  3. IT assesses which locations and services are affected and informs management.
  4. Recover network connectivity and priority systems, then restore servers and data from verified clean backups.
  5. Test database integrity, user access, security controls and branch connectivity before returning systems to normal use.
  6. Record the incident, communicate status and review lessons learned.

Prevention and preparedness

  • Back up critical databases and configurations on a schedule.
  • Keep a protected backup copy separate from production systems.
  • Monitor backup results and test restoration regularly.
  • Maintain network diagrams, supplier contacts, configuration records and recovery instructions in a secure location.
  • Use UPS equipment safely and follow approved shutdown procedures during extended power interruptions.
